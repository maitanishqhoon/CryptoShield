1. CryptoShield in Layman's Language: The Big Picture

The Real-World Problem

Modern ransomware has evolved into "Double Extortion":

  • The Theft (Exfiltration): First, the attackers quietly read and copy your private documents, pack them into an archive, and sneak them out over the network. They threaten to publish them publicly if you don't pay.
  • The Lockup (Encryption): Next, they run through your hard drive at lightning speed, scrambling every file into gibberish (encrypting them) and deleting your backups so you can't restore them.

The CryptoShield Analogy: A High-Tech Bank & Embassy

Imagine an embassy or a high-security bank. CryptoShield is not just a single lock on the front door; it is an integrated immune system:

  • The Tripwires (Canary Files): Fake stacks of money left on tables. If anyone so much as touches them, silent alarms blare.
  • The Security Guards & Cameras (Monitoring): Guards watching who touches what file, how fast they are opening drawers, and what programs are running.
  • The Suspicion Brain (Threat Scorer): Distinguishes a busy accountant from a masked burglar shoving files into bags.
  • The Blast Doors (Containment): The instant a thief is spotted, steel shutters drop over the vault door, and the suspect is disarmed and frozen.
  • The Border Customs Checkpoint (DLP & Exfiltration Gate): Even if a thief grabs documents, they cannot walk out the gate without customs inspecting every single box. Confidential files are stopped cold before a single byte leaves the building.
  • The Indestructible Underground Vault (Crypto Vault & Recovery): A hidden, sealed backup taken earlier. Even if a fire burns down the front desk, original documents are decrypted in staging, verified bit-for-bit, and put right back where they belong.
  • The Black Box & Notary Seal (Phase 9 — Forensic Reporting): After an incident, an automated investigator fuses every camera log, alarm, customs record, and recovery receipt into one tamper-evident dossier sealed with a cryptographic SHA-256 fingerprint.
  • The Command Center Wall (Phase 10 — PySide6 Desktop SOC Console): A unified dark-mode Security Operations Center where operators watch live threat gauges, throw emergency blast-door switches, verify forensic seals, and run safe attack simulations—all synced to one IST clock.

2. Technological Concepts Used (What They Mean & Why We Used Them)

Here is every major computer science, cryptographic, forensic, and desktop UI concept built into CryptoShield, explained in plain terms:

A. Cryptography & Forensic Integrity Concepts

Concept What It Is in Plain English Where & Why We Use It
AES-256-GCM
(Symmetric Authenticated Encryption)		 Think of this as an armored military lockbox. It scrambles data using a 256-bit key. GCM (Galois/Counter Mode) generates a cryptographic "tamper-evident seal" (the 128-bit Auth Tag). If an attacker changes even one single bit of the encrypted file on disk, GCM detects it immediately and refuses to open. Used by CryptoEngine to encrypt files into .cso containers for snapshots, and by RestorationManager to authenticate plaintext before commit.
RSA-3072 & RSA-OAEP
(Asymmetric Public/Private Encryption)		 A two-key system: a Public Key that can only lock the safe, and a Private Key needed to open it. OAEP with SHA-256 prevents mathematical pattern attacks. Used by KeyManager and SnapshotManager. Each backup snapshot gets its own random AES key, wrapped with RSA-OAEP so it cannot be opened without the master private key.
Argon2id
(Memory-Hard Key Derivation)		 A password hasher that deliberately consumes 64 MiB of RAM, 3 iterations, and 4 parallel lanes. It makes brute-force password guessing impractical, even on GPUs. Used in Envelope V2 (Argon2id-AES256GCM) to protect the master RSA private key on disk against offline brute-force cracking.
SHA-256
(Cryptographic Hash Function)		 A one-way mathematical fingerprint. Change a single byte in a file, and its 64-character hex fingerprint changes completely. Used in baseline_manifest.json, RecoveryAssessor, and RestorationManager to ensure every restored file matches its original 100%.
HMAC-SHA256
(Keyed-Hash Message Authentication)		 A digital signature created with a secret key proving a marker file was written by CryptoShield and has not been forged or moved. Used in .cryptoshield_lab_marker and .cryptoshield_recovery_marker. Prevents touching files outside designated safe lab or recovery folders.
Cryptographic Chain of Custody & Dossier Seal
(Phase 9)		 Freezing an entire incident dossier by computing a deterministic SHA-256 digest over canonical sorted JSON (sort_keys=True), binding the baseline manifest hash and quarantined evidence hashes. Used by ForensicDossier.compute_dossier_hash() and ReportExporter.verify_dossier_seal(). Editing even one metric or timestamp breaks the seal.

B. Operating System, Forensic & Desktop UI Concepts

Concept What It Is in Plain English Where & Why We Use It
Canary Files / Honeypots Deceptive "bait" files (e.g., passwords_master_backup.json, executive_salaries_2026.csv) placed quietly in directories. Normal users never touch them; ransomware inevitably hits them first. Used in CanaryAnalyzer and ReadBurstDetector. Touching a canary awards +50 to +60 threat points and raises emergency alerts.
File System Telemetry
(watchdog)		 Hooking into the OS filesystem notification layer to capture file creations, edits, renames, and deletions in real time. Used in FilesystemMonitor to track rapid file changes across the 20,000-file laboratory baseline.
Process Inspection
(psutil)		 Inspecting the OS process table for running programs, parent processes, PIDs, start times, and executable paths. Used in ProcessMonitor and ControlledProcessRegistry to attribute simulator activity accurately without guessing.
Sliding Time Windows Evaluating activity over rolling intervals (1s, 5s, 10s, 30s) rather than isolated snapshots. Used in BehaviorAnalyzer and ReadBurstDetector to distinguish normal edits from rapid ransomware bursts.
Pub/Sub Event Bus A central thread-safe broadcasting hub (app/core/event_bus.py). Subsystems emit typed events without tight coupling. Connects monitoring, detection, containment, exfiltration, DLP, recovery, Phase 9 auto-reporting, and Phase 10 live GUI updates.
PID Reuse Protection OSes recycle PIDs after processes exit. Checking PID alone risks stopping an innocent app started later. ControlledProcessRegistry checks PID + start-time + executable path + owned handle before ProcessController stops any simulator.
Pre-Transmission Gate
(DLP)		 Intercepting outbound transfers in memory before bytes reach the socket, enforcing ALLOW, ALERT, REQUIRE_APPROVAL, or BLOCK. Used in DLPTransferGate and DLPApprovalManager (APR-* tokens) to guarantee zero-byte leakage of restricted or canary data.
Forensic Aggregation & MITRE ATT&CK Mapping
(Phase 9)		 Fusing telemetry from detection, containment, recovery, DLP, and exfiltration into one chronological dossier with academic KPIs and MITRE tags (T1486, T1083, T1005, T1074, T1048). Implemented in ForensicAggregator and ReportExporter, exporting JSON, Markdown, CSV, and dark-theme HTML reports under runtime/reports/.
Thread-Safe Qt Signal Bridge & Async Workers
(Phase 10)		 Background threads cannot safely touch GUI widgets directly. A bridge converts EventBus events into Qt queued signals while QThread workers run heavy tasks off the UI thread. Implemented in EventBusBridge (app/ui/bridge.py) and AsyncWorker (app/ui/workers.py), keeping the 8-tab PySide6 console responsive.
Centralized Temporal Sync
(IST / UTC+05:30)		 Normalizing every clock, event timestamp, log line, and dossier entry to Indian Standard Time (IST). Implemented in app/core/time.py (now_ist(), format_ist(), parse_iso_to_ist()) so cross-subsystem timelines align to the microsecond.

4. The Complete End-to-End Workflow (Step-by-Step)

Here is how CryptoShield works end-to-end—both under the hood and live inside the PySide6 SOC Console (python -m app.main gui):

Step 1: Peacetime Preparation (The Immune Blueprint)

CryptoShield initializes runtime/lab/ with an HMAC-SHA256 .cryptoshield_lab_marker, synthesizes up to 20,000 enterprise files, compiles baseline_manifest.json, plants Canary honeypots, and creates an encrypted Vault Snapshot (SNAP-XXXX). In the GUI, the top ribbon reads LAB: VALID, GATE: UNLOCKED, THREAT: 0 NORMAL, and DLP: DEFAULT.

Step 2: Attack Phase 1 — Data Harvesting (The Theft)

An attacker reads sensitive files across Engineering/Research, HR, and Finance. ReadBurstDetector flags the multi-department read spike and broadcasts telemetry over EventBus to ExfiltrationCorrelator.

Step 3: Attack Phase 2 — Staging

Stolen documents are gathered and compressed into confidential_export.zip. StagingDetector detects the staging archive creation in real time.

Step 4: Attack Phase 3 — Exfiltration Blocked by DLP

The simulator attempts to stream the archive to the loopback MockReceiver (127.0.0.1). DLPTransferGate intercepts the transfer in memory before socket egress, inspects its classification/canary flags, halts it with zero bytes leaked, logs to runtime/dlp/audit.log, and updates Tab 6 (Exfiltration & DLP).

Step 5: Attack Phase 4 — Ransomware Burst & Canary Tripwire

Ransomware rapidly scrambles files and touches a canary. CanaryAnalyzer spikes threat points (+50/+60), BehaviorAnalyzer catches the write burst, and ThreatScorer hits CRITICAL (95–100/100). On the GUI Dashboard, Canaries flip to BREACHED, the ThreatGauge turns red, and the top ribbon shows THREAT: CRITICAL.

Step 6: Instant Containment

IncidentManager triggers ContainmentManager: LabWriteGate locks (.write_gate_state set to locked; ribbon turns red GATE: LOCKED), ProcessController verifies PID + start-time + path in ControlledProcessRegistry and stops the simulator cleanly, and ContainmentVerifier confirms zero post-containment writes.

Step 7: Cryptographic Restoration (The Cure)

RecoveryAssessor compares disk files against the snapshot manifest in parallel. Damaged files are quarantined as evidence. RestorationManager decrypts clean .cso objects into staging, verifies both the AES-GCM auth tag and SHA-256 digest, and atomically restores every file (tracked live on Tab 5's progress bar).

Step 8: Automated Forensic Dossier & Chain-of-Custody Seal (Phase 9)

When the incident resolves (SECURITY_INCIDENT_RESOLVED), ForensicCoordinator invokes ForensicAggregator and ReportExporter to compile a sealed ForensicDossier (REP-XXXXXXXX) under runtime/reports/ in JSON, Markdown, CSV, and HTML formats—complete with IST timelines, MITRE ATT&CK mappings, academic KPIs, and a dossier_sha256 seal.

Step 9: Operator Audit & Verification in the SOC Console (Phase 10)

In Tab 7 (Forensic Reports), the operator clicks Cryptographically Verify Seal ([PASS] VALID), reviews the Academic KPIs (0 Bytes Leaked, 100.0% SHA-256 match) and IST timeline, and runs Tab 2's Integrity Audit across all 20,000 baseline files (100% matched, 0 modified, 0 missing).
